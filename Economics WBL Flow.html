Economics work-based learning

This represents six entering classes, combining incoming spring and fall first-year students for clarity. In each class’s sophomore spring, they enter the grey pool of students who might start searching for WBL. This timing is based off the earliest they might begin their placements after completing CE-200.

Students either move up to their secured placement or into an “unsuccessful search” group, where they try again for a second time. If they are unsuccessful in that second search they move into an Experience Accelerator program through the Career Center, with intensive 1:1 coaching, alumni interaction, and other services to get them into a successful placement.

The top rail indicates students who graduate without a placement (0 under the model’s current default values). You can adjust the values below to model different scenarios.

Reading the chart. Each column is one term. The band along the bottom holds students who have not yet searched, the middle band holds those who searched and came up empty, and the four lanes above are placements made that term. A class arrives on the gold rail underneath and swoops up into the pool the term it becomes eligible. The window closes after senior spring, and whoever is still unplaced rises to the rail across the top and leaves.

Why the classes overlap. A class is eligible for seven terms and a new one becomes eligible every three, so two classes are always searching at once and in every third term a third class joins them. A term's placements are those classes combined. The first and last few terms are thinner because only one class is in play, so read the steady state from the middle of the chart rather than its ends.
